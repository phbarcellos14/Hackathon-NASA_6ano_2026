    Three simultaneous layers, not three sequential steps

    The layers are not a ladder. They run at the same time and have different roles. What changes by environment is who validates the decision — not which layers are available.

    LAYER 1 — AUTONOMOUS
    What the system releases without confirmation, because the risk of waiting is greater than the risk of acting. Low execution complexity, high urgency.
    Examples: Aspirin, O2, positioning, telemetry, capillary glucose.
    LAYER 2 — ASSISTED
    What requires correct execution and verification before administering: weight-based dose, renal function, interaction, allergy, stock, lot, expiry. The system guides step by step and the astronaut confirms. These are NOT secondary measures — this is the layer that allows safely performing what a physician would perform, without the physician in the loop in real time.
    Examples: clopidogrel, enoxaparin, atorvastatin, empirical antibiotic within 1 hour, antiarrhythmic.
    LAYER 3 — HANDOFF
    Asynchronous consultation with the flight surgeon using a structured 10-line summary. It is the decision channel when there is a link. It becomes a record for later validation when there is not.
    ⚠ Layer 2 is never switched off. On Mars it is more critical, not less: the empirical sepsis antibiotic must go in within 1 hour, and the flight surgeon is 24 minutes away.

    EVIDENCE NOTE — CPR IN MICROGRAVITY
    DGLRM/ESAM-SMG guideline (Scand J Trauma Resusc Emerg Med, 2020): in immediate BLS the method of choice is Evetts-Russomano; in ALS, with the patient restrained in the CMRS, the handstand method. Airway access only with two rescuers and a restrained patient. MACCC-0G study (Resuscitation, 2024): only the LUCAS 3 reached guideline-compliant depth (≥50 mm) in zero gravity; EASYPULSE and AUTOPULSE stayed at 29 mm. No automatic device has been tested in real spaceflight.
    In an emergency with a single conscious crew member, autonomous release is allowed and logged as such. The assisted layer exists so the astronaut does not have to improvise dose, interaction or renal adjustment when the flight surgeon cannot respond in real time.

    RED ALERT — IMMEDIATE ACTION
    Hemodynamic instability: BP 82/54, HR 128, heavy sweating, ST change in V2-V4.
    Reason for the system-generated alert, reviewed by the crew.
    Immediate action
    1. PATIENT RESTRAINT MANDATORY BEFORE STEP 2
      Secure the patient to the Crew Medical Restraint System (CMRS) before any compression. In microgravity, without restraint there is no effective compression.
    2. ⚡ ACTIVATE THE AED
      Locate the AED in the emergency kit. Turn it on and follow the voice prompts. Place the pads as shown in the chest diagram.
      USAGE GUIDELINES
      • ☐ Turn on the AED and follow the voice instructions. Do not touch the patient during rhythm analysis.
      • ☐ Dry the chest if wet. Remove medication patches (nitrate, nicotine, transdermal analgesia) from the pad site.
      • ☐ Place the pads: one below the right clavicle, the other on the left mid-axillary line. In microgravity, use the adhesive pads and press to ensure contact.
      • ☐ Confirm no one is touching the patient before the shock. Announce 'CLEAR' and check visually.
      • ☐ After the shock, resume compressions immediately. Do not wait for a pulse. 2-minute cycle until the next analysis.
      • ☐ If the rhythm is not shockable, the AED advises continuing compressions. Keep the device connected for re-analysis every 2 minutes.
      In microgravity, the patient must be restrained in the CMRS before the shock. A free body moves with the discharge and with compressions, compromising pad contact.
    3. START COMPRESSIONS
      Evetts-Russomano method if the patient is not yet restrained; handstand method after restraint in the CMRS. 2-minute cycle.
    4. AIRWAY — WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS
      • ✔ A second-generation supraglottic device is the airway of choice when no rescuer has extensive tracheal intubation training. Formal recommendation of the DGLRM/ESAM-SMG guideline.
      • ✔ Tracheal intubation by direct laryngoscopy in free float is NOT safe. npj Microgravity study (2023): even trained physicians did not reach the minimum first-attempt success standard.
      • ✔ Face-to-face videolaryngoscopy showed a 90.3% success rate versus 63.0% for the conventional method in microgravity (npj Microgravity, 2026).
      • ✔ Airway only with two rescuers present and the patient restrained in the CMRS.
      • ✔ Real training: the Crew Medical Officer receives about 80 hours of medical training. Most CMOs are not physicians. Skill loss during flight is a documented concern, and continuous training is considered essential.
      CAPNOGRAPHY — on-board availability not confirmed in the current configuration. Display as 'if available'.
    5. EPINEPHRINE AND CAPNOGRAPHY
      Epinephrine per protocol. Capnography if available.
    6. AUTOMATIC CHEST COMPRESSION
      If an automatic compression device is on board, install it without delaying manual compressions. Attach it to the CMRS.
      ATTACHMENT — IF AVAILABLE ON BOARD
      • 1. Confirm the patient is restrained in the CMRS. The device attaches to the patient-board assembly, not to the floor.
      • 2. Position the suction cup at the center of the sternum, at the same landmark used for manual compression.
      • 3. Snap the device arm into the CMRS mounting interface and engage the locks.
      • 4. Confirm alignment and no slack before activating. Slack reduces compression depth.
      • 5. Activate and verify: target depth ≥ 50 mm, rate 100 to 120 per minute.
      • 6. Monitor displacement every cycle. The MACCC-0G study identified displacement as the main failure of devices that did not reach adequate depth.
      • 7. Do not stop manual compressions until the device is activated and verified.
      Never delay manual compression to install the device. The guideline states that installation must not delay high-quality compressions.

    REALITY NOTE — TRAINING AND EQUIPMENT
    SpaceDoc does not assume the crew has advanced airway skills. The system guides what the rescuer can safely perform: supraglottic device instead of intubation, restraint before compression, and abstention when the resource is not on board. Each checklist item shows whether the resource is available in the mission configuration.
    WHY THIS SCREEN EXISTS SEPARATELY FROM SCREEN 10
    Screen 10 is the reasoning screen: there is time to read, compare layers and check the dose. This screen is the blind-execution screen: there is no time to read. A crew member in cardiac arrest does not browse protocol tabs. Here there is only the reason in large text, six steps operable with gloves and the sealed pharmacy. It is the equivalent of the laminated card on the lid of the crash cart.
